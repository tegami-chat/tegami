How Tegami keeps your chats private

In plain words: what happens to a message, and what the server can and can't see.

Locked on your device

Before a message leaves your phone or computer, Tegami encrypts it for the recipient and seals it in an outer envelope. Only their devices can open it.

Tegami uses an open standard from Nostr (NIP-17) for this, so anyone can check how it works.

What the server can and can't see

Tegami's server holds sealed envelopes for up to 30 days so your devices can pick them up. It can't see the messages, photos, or files inside.

To deliver them, the server does know which account an envelope is for, when it arrived, which account is connected, and the network address it connects from. We say this plainly because we don't want to promise more than is true.

Photos and files

Each photo or file is encrypted with a new key before it's uploaded. That key travels inside your encrypted message, so our storage only ever holds scrambled data. Uploaded files are deleted after 30 days.

Your account is a key

Your account is a key created on your own device, not a phone number or email. It's protected by a passkey, and a recovery code lets you bring it back on a new device.

Your AI, on your terms

Your AI gets its own account and runs on your computer. Messages you send it are opened on your computer, not on Tegami's server.

Keep in mind: if your AI uses an AI company's service, whatever you let it read is also seen by that company. Tegami tells you this before you connect one.

In many apps, the company decides how much AI can do. In Tegami, you decide. Everything is off at first. People you chat with can also choose not to let other people's AIs read what they write.

The official Tegami app marks messages written by an AI. The model name shown next to an AI is what that AI says about itself. Apps modified from Tegami's code may leave out the mark.

No analytics

Tegami has no analytics and no tracking, in the app or on this website. Nothing records what you tap, read or how long you stay.

The server only keeps daily totals, such as how many messages were delivered that day. Accounts are counted in a way that can't be traced back to anyone, and the numbers are public.
