Nostr, encryption and keys

Which parts of Nostr Tegami uses, how messages are sealed, what the relay can see, and how keys are kept.

Status labels such as “Available” are explained in the overview.

Relationship to Nostr

Section titled “Relationship to Nostr”

Is Tegami a Nostr client?

Section titled “Is Tegami a Nostr client?”

Now

Tegami is a messaging app built on the Nostr protocol, but it is not a Nostr client in the social-network sense. Accounts use Nostr keys (secp256k1) and messages are sent and received in the NIP-17 format, but Tegami never publishes public posts (kind 1) or a public profile (kind 0).

Can I message people who use other Nostr apps?

Section titled “Can I message people who use other Nostr apps?”

Now

No. The Tegami app connects only to its own relay (wss://relay.tegami.chat), and that relay only accepts envelopes from connections logged in with a key registered with Tegami. We keep it closed so spam and impersonation are stopped at the door. On the relay side we have registration control, sending limits, reports, and the ability for us to suspend keys.

Not yet

Tegami does not offer one of Nostr’s core strengths: using the same key in any app.

Next

  • Under discussion We are comparing a few options: letting you add a company relay as an inbox, showing messages from other Nostr apps, and listing in an official directory only relays that agree to Tegami’s rules. We have not picked one yet.

Can I log in with my existing nsec? Can I export my Tegami key?

Section titled “Can I log in with my existing nsec? Can I export my Tegami key?”

Now

No to both. Tegami generates your key inside the app and never lets it leave.

Not yet

If Tegami goes away, there is no way to move to another app with the same key.

Next

  • Under discussion As an escape hatch in case Tegami goes away, we are considering letting you export your key. Exporting would let you move to another app, but if an exported key is stolen your account can be taken over, and publishing a profile in another app would link your public key to your identity.
  • Under discussion For people who want to use other Nostr apps with the same key while still using Tegami, we are also considering having the Tegami app sign on their behalf without handing over the secret key (NIP-46). NIP-46 only works while the Tegami app is running, though, so it is not an escape hatch.

Which parts of Nostr does Tegami use, and which does it not?

Section titled “Which parts of Nostr does Tegami use, and which does it not?”

Now

Nostr feature In Tegami
Account = secp256k1 key pair (npub and nsec) Used. No phone number or email address
Event format (id, pubkey, created_at, kind, tags, content, sig) and Schnorr signatures Used. Messages, relay login, and signatures for HTTP endpoints all use this format
Relays (servers that store and deliver events over WebSocket) Used. But only one Tegami-only relay, not public relays
Encrypted DMs (NIP-17, NIP-44, NIP-59) Used. The foundation of messaging
Public posts (kind 1), follows (kind 3), timelines Not used. No social-network features
Public profile (kind 0) Not used. Your name is passed in the invite link, and your icon is delivered encrypted to friends only
Published relay lists (NIP-65 kind 10002, NIP-17 kind 10050) Not used. The app connects only to the Tegami relay
Zaps (Bitcoin tips) Not used in the app. Supported only for receiving on the official account
Legacy DMs (NIP-04) Not used. They expose sender and recipient to the relay

In short, Tegami uses Nostr as a way to “prove identity with a key and leave signed envelopes with a relay,” and leaves out the social-network parts.

Next

  • Under discussion We want people to be able to send money freely with Bitcoin. We have not decided whether to build Zaps into the app.

Which NIPs does Tegami use?

Section titled “Which NIPs does Tegami use?”

Now

NIP Usage
NIP-17, NIP-59 One-to-one and group messages. Text uses kind 14, photos and files use kind 15, wrapped in a seal (kind 13) and gift wrap (kind 1059)
NIP-44 Encryption of seals and gift wraps, and of device transfer data
NIP-42 Authentication (login) to the relay. Both reads and writes require an authenticated key
NIP-40 Expiring envelopes (1 minute for call signals, 10 minutes for device transfer and AI draft requests)
NIP-98 Signatures for HTTP endpoints (registration, notifications, recovery backups, reports, admin operations)
NIP-05, NIP-11 Identifier for the official account (hello@tegami.chat), plus relay info and the running version
Blossom (kind 24242 signatures) Storage for photos and files (/upload and /<sha256>). Data is stored and fetched by the sha256 of the encrypted bytes. /stamp for stickers is a Tegami-specific extension

Where no NIP covers what we need, we use our own kinds, only inside encrypted envelopes: icon (10100), sticker list (10101), call signaling (25050), and AI draft requests and replies (25060, 25061).

Next

  • Available We added more custom kinds: sync between your own devices (10102), group member changes (10103), consent to invite an AI into a chat (10104), and exchanges with AI (25062 to 25070). In this version the app does not use 10104 or the AI-to-AI messages (25066, 25067) yet.
  • Under discussion If the proposals for key rotation and protecting keys with passkeys (NIP pull requests #2137 and #2519) are merged, we will consider adopting them.

Encryption and what the server can see

Section titled “Encryption and what the server can see”

What can the server see?

Section titled “What can the server see?”

Now

The relay cannot read message contents. All the relay can see is:

  • The recipient’s public key on each envelope (the gift wrap’s p tag), the time it arrived, and its size
  • The keys logged in and connected, and the IP address they connect from
  • Tags on the outside of the envelope: whether it is a call (t=call), whether it should not trigger a notification (t=quiet), and its expiry (expiration)
  • Beyond envelopes: who invited whom at registration (deleted after 90 days), the keys that stored stickers and their sizes, and the contents of reports (deleted after 90 days)

A gift wrap hides the sender by itself, but because the Tegami relay requires login for writes too, at the moment it accepts an envelope it knows which key sent it to which key. However, the relay code does not store the sender’s key (in relay/src/index.ts, the events table has no sender column). The one exception: envelopes sent by people who registered without an invite code are stored with a marker, so the app shows them only when the sender is already a friend.

Not yet

There is no way for anyone outside to verify that the running relay is the same as the published code. The relay reports its running commit in /stats and in the NIP-11 version, but that is only our own claim.

Next

  • Under discussion We have not yet decided how to let outsiders verify that the running relay matches the published code.

Does Tegami have forward secrecy?

Section titled “Does Tegami have forward secrecy?”

Now

No. NIP-44 derives its encryption key from the long-term keys of the sender and the recipient, so if a secret key is stolen, envelopes still on the relay (up to 30 days) and messages stored on devices can be decrypted later. Only the outer gift wrap layer uses a fresh throwaway key each time. Tegami protects your secret key by encrypting it with a passkey (or, where PRF is not supported, with a non-extractable key on the device), making the key itself harder to steal.

Not yet

Compared with schemes like Signal or MLS that keep rotating keys, this is a weakness.

Next

  • Under discussion We have not decided whether to move to a scheme that keeps rotating keys.

How are groups encrypted?

Section titled “How are groups encrypted?”

Now

As the NIP-17 spec describes, we build a separate envelope for each member. For example, one message to a group of you plus 5 others becomes 6 envelopes, including your own copy. The relay cannot tell who is in the same group.

Not yet

The number of envelopes grows with group size, so this does not suit large groups. We do not use MLS.

Next

  • Available Groups now have a stable identifier, and adding, removing, and leaving members, name and icon changes, and admin changes are sent to everyone as changes carrying the signature (seal) of the person who made them. You can choose between groups with admins and neutral groups.
  • Under discussion We would also like to build spaces where many people can gather, like Discord servers.

In the browser version, isn’t it game over if the server serves malicious JavaScript?

Section titled “In the browser version, isn’t it game over if the server serves malicious JavaScript?”

Now

Yes. This is a weakness shared by every encrypted app that runs in a browser: if the source that serves it (app.tegami.chat on Cloudflare) is tampered with, it could serve code that steals keys. That is why Tegami sets a strict CSP that limits where code can load from, and includes no third-party analytics scripts at all.

Not yet

There is no way to verify that the files we serve were built from the public repository (reproducible builds plus verification).

Next

  • Decided We will ship apps for iPhone, Android, Windows, Mac, and Linux. Versions distributed through app stores are fixed, which makes this weakness smaller.
  • Coming soon The Android app runs on a test device (emulator) as far as showing the chat list.

Keys

Section titled “Keys”

How is my secret key protected?

Section titled “How is my secret key protected?”

Now

We use the passkey PRF extension to get a fixed 32-byte value per passkey, derive an AES-GCM key from it, and encrypt your Nostr secret key with that. Only the ciphertext is stored on the device, and every time you open the app it asks for Face ID or Windows Hello. If your passkey store does not support PRF, you can instead choose to keep a non-extractable AES key in IndexedDB and encrypt with that. The passkey stores we have confirmed working are iCloud Keychain and Windows Hello.

Not yet

Bitwarden can store passkeys but does not return PRF values, so it cannot be used to protect keys with a passkey. Passkeys are also not supported in the iPhone and Android apps.

Next

  • Decided We will support Bitwarden. Tegami follows the PRF spec, so once Bitwarden returns PRF values, it will work without any changes on our side.
  • Decided We will keep using passkeys, not passwords, to protect your key.

What happens if I lose my device?

Section titled “What happens if I lose my device?”

Now

You can restore with a 28-character (140-bit) recovery code created in Settings. From this code, HKDF derives three things: a backup ID, an encryption key, and a signing key. Your secret key, name, friends, groups, blocks, AI permissions, and sticker list (up to 100) are encrypted into a backup stored on the relay. The backup is signed with a key derived from the recovery code, so the relay cannot tell whose account it belongs to.

Not yet

If you have not created a recovery code and lose all your devices, we cannot restore your account either. Chat history is not restored by the recovery code.

Next

  • Decided Users will be able to choose how to recover a lost key.
  • Under discussion For stolen keys, we are considering a way to move your connections to a new key by having friends you have verified in person vouch for the new key.

Can I move to a new device?

Section titled “Can I move to a new device?”

Now

Yes, with a device transfer link. Your old device creates a throwaway key, encrypts your secret key, name, friends, groups, blocks, AI permissions, and sticker list with NIP-44 so that only that key can open it, and leaves it on the relay as an envelope that expires in 10 minutes. The throwaway secret key is included only after the # in the link, so it never reaches the server. Your chat history moves too (see below), and the new device also re-fetches the envelopes still on the relay from the last 30 days (up to 500 at a time).

Next

  • Available The device transfer link also moves your chats to the new device. Chats are compressed, encrypted, and passed through the same storage as photos (up to about 32MB before compression, newest first, so very old messages may stay on the old device). Photos and files themselves are not moved; they open on the new device only while they are still in storage.
  • Available Syncing your friend list, name, blocks, and AI permissions between your own devices is also implemented.
