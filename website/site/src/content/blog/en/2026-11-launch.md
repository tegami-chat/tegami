On November 11, 2026, I started building a messaging app called Tegami.

For years, I have hated opening a certain well-known messaging app. Every time I open it, I see ads I don't care about and news I don't want to read. The screen is full of shopping features I have never once used. All I want is to message my family and friends. Yet every time, the app annoys me with useless information (and that's on a good day).

Still, a lot of people use that app. These days, even restaurant ordering and local government notices go through it. Not using it isn't really an option. I would happily switch to Telegram, iMessage, or anything else. But I can't, because nobody around me uses them. I have felt this way for years.

Meanwhile, AI has improved enormously over the past few years, and I started building apps as a hobby. AI is evolving at an incredible pace, and while it scares me a little, I've been having a lot of fun using it. For people like me who use AI every day, it has become the entry point to software and the internet. So it is natural to want to read and reply to messages from family and friends through AI as well. Some people work around this with an app like Beeper, which brings multiple messaging apps together in one place. I figured I might as well build my own messaging app.

Tegami is an open-source messaging app. The name comes from the Japanese word *tegami*, which means "letter." A letter is sealed in an envelope, and only the person who wrote it and the person who reads it know what it says. I believe messages in an app should work the same way.

Tegami does not make money from personal data, and it does not show ads. Messages are encrypted on your device before they are sent, so even I, the person running the service, cannot read them. Under the hood, Tegami uses Nostr, a protocol for sending messages without relying on any single company.

If you want an open-source messaging app with no ads, where the operator cannot read your messages, Signal already exists. There is also 0xchat, a messaging app built on Nostr. But I have never seen anyone in Japan using either of them. So I think building AI into Tegami can make it a messaging app that is a little different from those apps, yet one that many people will want to use.

I've only just started building Tegami, and a lot is still missing. The web version is available at https://app.tegami.chat. It's still pretty rough around the edges (sorry about that), but please give it a try.

If you notice anything while using it, please let me know by email at hello@tegami.chat.
