How the secret key is protected, what happens if you lose a device, moving to a new device, and importing and exporting keys.

Status labels such as "Available" are explained in the [overview](/developers/#what-the-status-labels-mean).

## How the secret key is protected

<Aside type="tip" title="Now">

We use the passkey PRF extension to get a fixed 32-byte value per passkey, derive an AES-GCM key from it, and encrypt your Nostr secret key with that. Only the ciphertext is stored on the device, and every time you open the app it asks for Face ID or Windows Hello. If your passkey store does not support PRF, you can instead choose to keep a non-extractable AES key in IndexedDB and encrypt with that. The passkey stores we have confirmed working are iCloud Keychain and Windows Hello.

</Aside>

<Aside type="caution" title="Not yet">

Bitwarden can store passkeys but does not return PRF values, so it cannot be used to protect keys with a passkey. Passkeys are also not supported in the iPhone and Android apps.

</Aside>

<Aside type="note" title="Next">

-   <Badge text="Decided" variant="note" />  We will support Bitwarden. Tegami follows the PRF spec, so once Bitwarden returns PRF values, it will work without any changes on our side.
-   <Badge text="Decided" variant="note" />  We will keep using passkeys, not passwords, to protect your key.

</Aside>

## If you lose your device

<Aside type="tip" title="Now">

You can restore with a 28-character (140-bit) recovery code created in Settings. From this code, HKDF derives three things: a backup ID, an encryption key, and a signing key. Your secret key, name, friends, groups, blocks, AI permissions, and sticker list (up to 100) are encrypted into a backup stored on the relay. The backup is signed with a key derived from the recovery code, so the relay cannot tell whose account it belongs to.

</Aside>

<Aside type="caution" title="Not yet">

If you have not created a recovery code and lose all your devices, we cannot restore your account either. Chat history is not restored by the recovery code.

</Aside>

<Aside type="note" title="Next">

-   <Badge text="Decided" variant="note" />  Users will be able to choose how to recover a lost key.
-   <Badge text="Under discussion" variant="caution" />  For stolen keys, we are considering a way to move your connections to a new key by having friends you have verified in person vouch for the new key.

</Aside>

## Moving to a new device

<Aside type="tip" title="Now">

Yes, with a device transfer link. Your old device creates a throwaway key, encrypts your secret key, name, friends, groups, blocks, AI permissions, and sticker list with NIP-44 so that only that key can open it, and leaves it on the relay as an envelope that expires in 10 minutes. The throwaway secret key is included only after the `#` in the link, so it never reaches the server. Your chat history moves too (see below), and the new device also re-fetches the envelopes still on the relay from the last 30 days (up to 500 at a time).

</Aside>

<Aside type="note" title="Next">

-   <Badge text="Available" variant="success" />  The device transfer link also moves your chats to the new device. Chats are compressed, encrypted, and passed through the same storage as photos (up to about 32MB before compression, newest first, so very old messages may stay on the old device). Photos and files themselves are not moved; they open on the new device only while they are still in storage.
-   <Badge text="Available" variant="success" />  Syncing your friend list, name, blocks, and AI permissions between your own devices is also implemented.

</Aside>

## Importing an nsec and exporting your key

<Aside type="tip" title="Now">

No to both. Tegami generates your key inside the app and never lets it leave.

</Aside>

<Aside type="caution" title="Not yet">

If Tegami goes away, there is no way to move to another app with the same key.

</Aside>

<Aside type="note" title="Next">

-   <Badge text="Under discussion" variant="caution" />  As an escape hatch in case Tegami goes away, we are considering letting you export your key. Exporting would let you move to another app, but if an exported key is stolen your account can be taken over, and publishing a profile in another app would link your public key to your identity.
-   <Badge text="Under discussion" variant="caution" />  For people who want to use other Nostr apps with the same key while still using Tegami, we are also considering having the Tegami app sign on their behalf without handing over the secret key (NIP-46). NIP-46 only works while the Tegami app is running, though, so it is not an escape hatch.

</Aside>
