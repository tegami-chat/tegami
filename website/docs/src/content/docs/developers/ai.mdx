How your own AI joins Tegami, what it can read, and what goes to the AI provider. To connect your own AI, see [Connect your own AI](/developers/connect-ai/).

Status labels such as "Available" are explained in the [overview](/developers/#what-the-status-labels-mean).

## How AI connects

<Aside type="tip" title="Now">

No, it does not run on our servers. When you run `tegami-agent` (Node.js) on your own PC, the AI gets its own Nostr key and talks over the same relay with the same encryption as human users. By default, replies are written by Claude Code on your PC (`claude -p`). With `--command` you can swap in another program that reads standard input and outputs a reply. Your AI subscription and login stay on your PC and are never passed to Tegami. It also runs as an MCP server, so you can ask Claude Code to "send it on Tegami."

</Aside>

<Aside type="caution" title="Not yet">

Connecting requires typing commands. Claude Code is the only AI that works without further setup. For any other AI (OpenAI, Gemini, OpenRouter, Ollama and other local models), run one setup command and give your own API key, or pass your own program to `--command`.

</Aside>

<Aside type="note" title="Next">

-   <Badge text="Available" variant="success" />  You can now add an AI as a friend just by scanning the QR code shown on your PC with the app.
-   <Badge text="Available" variant="success" />  Replies can also be written by local models (such as Ollama) or with your own API key (Anthropic, OpenAI, Gemini, OpenRouter).
-   <Badge text="Available" variant="success" />  AI can now send messages in its owner's name. In this version, only the mode where the owner reviews each message before it is sent is available. Sending without asking will come in a later version.

</Aside>

## Protection against prompt injection

<Aside type="tip" title="Now">

Three mechanisms guard against this:

-   The AI only replies automatically to people its owner has allowed (`--trust`). For messages from people who are not friends, the AI never sees the content; it is only told how many people have sent new messages.
-   Even when you let the AI read a chat with a friend, it only happens when you allow it with a switch in the app, and then only the latest 30 messages of that one-to-one chat are handed to the AI in an envelope that expires in 10 minutes. The AI only writes a draft; you are the one who sends it.
-   That switch lives in the app, which holds the owner's key. If the switch were on the PC where the AI runs, the AI could change the setting itself.

</Aside>

<Aside type="caution" title="Not yet">

Anything you let the AI read also goes to the AI provider. If the PC has no OS keystore, the AI's secret key is saved unencrypted only when you add `--insecure-store`.

</Aside>

<Aside type="note" title="Next">

-   <Badge text="Available" variant="success" />  The other person can now choose "Don't let others' AI read my messages," and mark individual messages "Don't let AI read." Disappearing messages are not passed to AI either. This works because the official app checks the mark and does not hand the message to AI, so it cannot stop modified apps or manual copying.
-   <Badge text="Available" variant="success" />  The AI's secret key and API keys are now stored encrypted in the OS keystore (verified on Windows; not yet verified on Mac and Linux).
-   <Badge text="Available" variant="success" />  We added limits on how much an AI can send, and any message containing a link must be confirmed by the owner. "Turn all off" lets you stop all your AIs at once from your phone.
-   <Badge text="Available" variant="success" />  Limits on what an AI sends: the app lets it send without asking 5 messages a minute, 20 in a row in one chat and 100 a day by default (you can change them, up to 60, 200 and 1,000). The relay allows one AI key 30 envelopes a minute and 600 a day, and all of one owner's AIs together 600 a day. A message to a group with five other people is six envelopes.

</Aside>

## Tools the AI can use and checks before sending

Claude Code's built-in tools and any MCP servers the owner added are off by default. Anything sent to someone other than the owner goes through a rule check and a model check first. Even so, the checks can't catch every secret or risky message.
